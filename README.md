# Arena Racer

A small online multiplayer racer built in Unity 6 with **Photon Fusion 2 (Shared mode)**. It plays in the browser (WebGL).

**Play:** https://huzafazahoor.github.io/games/arena-racer/
**Two clients side by side:** https://huzafazahoor.github.io/games/arena-racer/duo.html opens two real Fusion connections in one private room. The right-hand client's car drives itself, and the left-hand one joins mid-race by taking over a bot.

- Six cars, three laps, about two minutes a race. Bots fill every empty seat, so pressing **Race** starts a race straight away.
- Open the page in a second window, or send it to a friend, and the new player **takes over a bot's car mid-race**.
- Press **Tab** for the **Dev View**, a live picture of the netcode. It shows remote cars' newest snapshot and extrapolated position as ghosts, where other players see *your* car, who simulates each car, ping, tick rate, measured packet rates, interpolation delay, bandwidth, and a timeline of snapshots arriving.

## The three files worth reading

| File | What it shows |
|---|---|
| [`NetCar.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Scripts/Car/NetCar.cs) | One networked car. Its State Authority (the player driving, or the master client for a bot) simulates it every tick. Everyone else interpolates between snapshots with Fusion's `PropertyReader`, snapping over resets. Car-to-car contact is resolved against where a remote car is *now* (extrapolated by the snapshot's age), not where its last snapshot was. Taking over a car is just a State Authority transfer. |
| [`RaceDirector.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Scripts/Race/RaceDirector.cs) | Race rules and room bookkeeping on a `MasterClientObject`. It hands bot cars to joining players, reclaims cars whose driver left, and makes a new master adopt the bots when the master leaves. It also runs the race state machine. Race time is counted on the shared network tick, and line crossings are timed to a fraction of a tick. |
| [`DevView.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Scripts/UI/DevView.cs) | The netcode visualiser. It draws ghosts, authority labels, the ping graph and the interpolation timeline from Fusion's own numbers (`RemoteRenderTime`, `LastReceiveTick`, `GetPlayerRtt`, statistics snapshots). |

Also of interest: [`CarPhysics.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Scripts/Car/CarPhysics.cs) is the deterministic arcade car model, with drift and mini-turbo. [`BotDriver.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Scripts/Car/BotDriver.cs) is the pure-pursuit bot with overtaking and stuck recovery. [`RacingLine.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Scripts/Track/RacingLine.cs) computes an elastic-band racing line and a braking-aware speed profile.

## How the networking works

- **Shared mode.** Each client has State Authority over what it drives and simulates it locally from its own input. Nobody waits on a server round trip to steer. The shared-mode **master client** also simulates the bots and owns the race director.
- **Bots are just unowned cars.** A joining player sends `RPC_RequestCar`. The master writes `Driver = player` on a bot car (the last-placed bot, if a race is on). The player then calls `RequestStateAuthority()`, and the car carries on from exactly where it was. The car prefab has `AllowStateAuthorityOverride` and not `DestroyWhenStateAuthorityLeaves`. When a player disconnects, their car's authority clears and the master claims it back as a bot.
- **Master migration.** The director is a `MasterClientObject`, so Fusion moves it to the next master automatically. The new master then notices that the bots belong to a player who has left and claims them.
- **Remote cars** are drawn at Fusion's remote render time, interpolated between the two snapshots around it. A `TeleportCount` counter stops interpolation across resets.
- **Pausing a shared race.** If you're the only human, the race freezes, and every tick-based timer is shifted when it resumes. With other humans in the room, a bot drives your car until you resume.
- **Offline fallback.** With no App ID or no connection, the same code runs in Fusion's `GameMode.Single`.

What a test session from Pakistan to the EU region showed in the Dev View: about 180 ms RTT, 32 Hz ticks and 16 Hz sends (Shared mode's rates). Remote cars are drawn about 300 ms behind local time. Bandwidth was about 5 kB/s in for a non-master, and about 4 kB/s out for the master, which simulates five bots and its own car. When the master's tab closed, the other client became master and adopted all five bots within about 3 seconds, and the race carried on.

## Building

- Unity **6000.0.58f2**. Fusion 2.0.6 is included under `Assets/Photon`.
- The scene, prefabs, materials and settings are generated by [`ProjectSetup.cs`](Assets/ArenaRacer/Editor/ProjectSetup.cs) (menu **Arena Racer → Rebuild Scene and Prefabs**). The sounds are synthesised by [`Tools/make_sounds.py`](Tools/make_sounds.py). There are no third-party art or audio assets: everything is generated.
- Online play needs a free Photon Fusion App ID from https://dashboard.photonengine.com. Put it in `photon-appid.txt` next to this README. The file is git-ignored, and the build script injects it only for the duration of the build.
- WebGL build (gzip + decompression fallback, for GitHub Pages):

```
Unity.exe -batchmode -nographics -quit -projectPath . -buildTarget WebGL -executeMethod PortfolioBuild.BuildWebGL -outPath Builds/arena-racer
```

## Controls

W/S/A/D or the arrow keys drive. Hold **Space** to drift through a corner and release it for a boost. **R** resets you onto the track, **Esc** pauses, **Tab** opens the Dev View and **M** mutes. A gamepad works too: stick to steer, RT/LT for throttle and brake, RB or X to drift, Start to pause, Back for the Dev View.
